The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

Start Thy Purse to Fattening

      The first cure teaches the importance of saving a portion of every income received. Arkad explains that a person should keep at least one-tenth of what they earn rather than spending everything. The idea is that a person’s purse cannot become full if all of their earnings immediately leave it.
      As a college student, I may not always have a large or consistent income. However, this principle reminds me that saving does not have to begin when I become wealthy. I can begin with whatever amount I am able to put aside from money I receive from work, allowances or other legitimate sources of income. I can also create a separate savings account or savings goal so that the money is not easily spent on things that are not necessary.

Control Thy Expenditures

      The second cure teaches that people must distinguish between their needs and their wants. The book explains that people’s desires can expand until they consume their entire income if they are not controlled. A budget therefore helps a person decide where their money should go.
      I have different expenses such as transportation, food, school supplies, academic expenses, communication and personal needs. I also have things that I may want but do not necessarily need. This lesson encourages me to create a realistic budget and prioritize my responsibilities before spending money on entertainment or unnecessary purchases. It also teaches me to avoid spending simply because I have money available.

Make Thy Gold Multiply

      The third cure explains that saving money is only the beginning. Once money has been saved, it should be put to productive use so that it can generate additional income. Arkad describes this as making one’s money work and produce further wealth.
     The book gives the example of Arkad investing money with a shield maker and receiving earnings from the investment. The lesson is that accumulated money can potentially become a source of additional income rather than remaining unused.
      This is especially meaningful to me because I am interested in building a successful career and becoming financially independent. Instead of thinking only about spending the money I earn, I should think about ways to use some of it to build my future.
     For example, after establishing an emergency fund and learning about an investment carefully, I could consider suitable long-term investments. However, I also understand from the later chapters that investment should not be based on excitement or promises of getting rich quickly.

Guard Thy Treasures from Loss

     The fourth cure focuses on protecting money from unnecessary risk. The book warns against investing without understanding the investment or accepting advice from people who are not knowledgeable in the area. Arkad describes losing his savings after trusting a brickmaker to purchase jewels for him.
     This lesson is particularly relevant in today’s environment because people are constantly exposed to investment opportunities through social media and the internet. Some opportunities may sound attractive because they promise quick or unusually large returns.
     As I become financially independent, I should not invest simply because a friend, influencer or stranger tells me that something is profitable. I should research the opportunity, understand the risks and seek advice from knowledgeable and trustworthy sources.

Make of Thy Dwelling a Profitable Investment

      The fifth cure encourages people to consider home ownership and productive use of their living arrangements. In the context of the book, Arkad argues that owning a suitable home can provide financial and personal benefits.
      At my current stage of life, owning a home is not an immediate priority because I am still pursuing my education and developing my career. However, this chapter encourages me to think about future assets rather than only short-term consumption.
      In the future, I would like to make careful decisions about property and other assets based on affordability, income and long-term value rather than purchasing something simply to appear successful.

Insure a Future Income

      The sixth cure emphasizes preparing for the future. Arkad explains that people should prepare for the period when they are older and less able to work, while also considering the financial protection of their families.
      As a young college student, retirement may seem very far away, but this chapter taught me that early preparation can make future financial responsibilities easier.
      My long-term goal should be to develop a career that provides stable income while also building savings and investments that can support me later in life. I also want to be in a position where unexpected financial situations do not completely disrupt my life.

Increase Thy Ability to Earn

      The seventh cure focuses on personal development and increasing one’s earning ability. Arkad explains that people can increase their income by improving their skills, knowledge, discipline and effectiveness. The book emphasizes having clear desires and continuously developing one’s abilities.
      This lesson is particularly relevant to me as a college student. My education is not simply about obtaining a qualification; it is also an opportunity to develop skills that can increase my future earning potential.
      I am studying Computer Sciences, so I should continue developing my knowledge, communication skills, leadership abilities, technological skills and practical experience. I should also look for opportunities such as internships, professional certifications and work experience that can make me more competitive in the job market.

The Five Laws Of Gold

      The Five Laws Of Gold provides another perspective on managing wealth. The book explains that gold comes to people who save, grows when wisely employed and is protected when managed carefully. It also warns against unfamiliar investments and unrealistic promises of quick wealth.

Gold Comes to Those Who Save

      The reinforces the first sure for a lean purse, I understand that saving is not supposed to be something I do only when I have extra money but it should be a regular financial habit.
      As I work towards financial independence, I want to develop the discipline of paying myself first. Even if my income may be small at first, establishing this habit will build the foundation in helping me to be financially disciplined as my income increases.

Gold Works for the Wise Owner

      This teaches me that building wealth involves more than just accumulating money. As long as I am able to establish sufficient savings, I should learn responsible ways about making my money productive.
      In the future, I want to fully understand investments before actually takin part in them. I should openly learn about all possible risk and returns factors rather than investing purely on emotions.

Gold stays with a Cautious Owner

      This law reminds me that asking for advice is not a weakness. Financial decisions can have serious consequence, therefore learning from experienced and trustworthy professionals can reduce avoidable mistakes.
      When I eventually have significant savings to invest, I should not assume that I automatically know everything. I should still seek advice, do research and seek mentoring from qualified financial professionals.

Gold Leaves Those Who Invest in What They Do Not Understand

      This is one of the most practical lessons in the book. Sometimes an opportunity can look profitable without the person fully understanding how it works.
     I should avoid investing simply because something is popular or because someone says it is guaranteed to make money. If I cannot explain how an investment works, how it makes money and what could cause me to lose money, I should learn more before committing my funds.

Gold Flees Those Seeking Impossible Earnings

      This law is especially relevant today because people are constantly exposed to advertisements and online personalities promising fast financial success. The book reminds me that sustainable wealth generally requires knowledge, patience and discipline.
      As I work toward becoming financially successful, I should avoid allowing greed or impatience to control my financial decisions. I want to build wealth gradually through education, employment, saving and informed investment rather than looking for shortcuts.

Reflection

      After studying the Seven Cures for a Lean Purse and the Five Laws of Gold, I realized that the book’s central message is not simply about money. It is also about discipline, knowledge, patience, responsibility and personal development.
      The principles can be applied to my life as a student because the financial habits I develop now can influence my future. Saving a portion of my income, controlling unnecessary spending, improving my skills, learning about investments and protecting my money are all habits that can contribute to greater financial responsibility.
      One of the most meaningful ideas in the book is that wealth does not begin when a person becomes rich; it begins with the habits that eventually allow a person to become financially secure.
      The book also changed the way I think about education. My degree is not only something that I need to complete. It is part of my preparation for increasing my ability to earn and creating opportunities for myself in the future.
      The greatest lesson I take from the book is that wealth is built through consistent decisions, not one-time opportunities.
